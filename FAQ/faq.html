Shipwreck Finder FAQs

Our coordinates are sourced from official hydrographic offices, historical naval logs, and verified marine archaeological databases. While many near-shore wrecks are highly accurate, deep-ocean wrecks may have a variance of a few hundred meters due to historical tracking limitations.

It depends entirely on the depth. Wrecks in shallow coastal waters (under 15 meters) can often be enjoyed with basic snorkeling gear. However, deeper wrecks require advanced scuba certification, and some deep-sea anomalies can only be viewed via our digital sonar maps or ROVs (Remotely Operated Vehicles).

Most shipwrecks are protected by international maritime law or local government heritage acts. While recreational diving is permitted at many sites, removing artifacts, disturbing the structure, or salvaging material without a permit is strictly illegal and subject to heavy fines.

Our database is updated monthly. We constantly integrate new sonar scan data from oceanographic expeditions, newly declassified naval records, and crowd-sourced verifications from certified diving communities.
